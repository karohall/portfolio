Concept

Mission ASS2ROID started with a bicep curl and a bad joke and got a life of its own.

The project evolved into a chaotic sci-fi universe complete with cinematic trailer, a rover roid bar, an ad campaign, and a guidebook.

ASS2ROID is a theme camp for The Borderland, a co-created festival following the Burning Man 10+1 principles. The premise: two prospectors are running a full mining operation on the fictional asteroid Korova Rock. Everything that follows is built from that premise outward, with total sincerity and absolutely no restraint. Imagine The Fifth Element if Luc Besson had made slightly worse decisions, which would have made it considerably better.

The project began as a collaborative creative exercise (while lifting heavy) between two people who watched Blade Runner at an impressionable age and never fully recovered. Combining a shared love of sci-fi franchises and 1980s comics, we went all-in with bleached blonde hair to the tunes of a soundtrack with over 100 songs.

Visual Direction

Pulpy, hand-made, easter eggs

The visual language draws from 1980s comic book artists, Richard Corben and Moebius in particular. The deliberate choice was to use AI tools (Midjourney, ElevenLabs) to achieve a hand-crafted, pulpy aesthetic rather than the smooth, clean output AI usually produces. Every frame of the trailer and every poster element should feel like it is part of an alternate comic book universe.

The fictional publisher "Kroeber Institute" lends institutional legitimacy to the lore. The Latin mission motto in profundum imis ("we go deep") is stitched onto the mission emblem with full seriousness.

Graphic Design

Two fonts. One pivot.

The initial visual direction leaned retrofuturistic with clean geometry — led by Lucidity Condensed, the kind of type that says space program rather than asteroid crime. Halfway through the project, that font didn't cut it. ASS2ROID had, if possible, grown into a mildly unhinged beast that needed something faster, something that could match its wildly ironic sex appeal.

The switch was to a grittier register: cyber grunge and Y2K aesthetics with a nod to Y3K and other influences from the algos, digital letterforms and a pinch of the visual grammar of something photocopied one too many times or printed on a dot-matrix printer in a truck stop on the edge of the solar system. The type needed to feel used. Like it had been somewhere, yet came from a cryo pod with no connection to vintage glamour. Enter Was Gayji NP, Was Don Chedi, and Reesha.

The final typographic palette carries that tension, hopefully institutional enough to hold the Kroeber Institute fiction together yet rough enough that you'd believe it was produced under pressure, in bad lighting, by people who hadn't slept. We call that Tuesday.

The Physical Build

Analogue art car

The physical iteration of the ASS2ROID concept is a kit-bashed and heavily retrofitted garden cart transformed into a roid rover bar — an analogue art car, if you will. We haunted local secondhand stores collecting old game consoles, VHS tapes, and other trinkets, and added custom tubing, a speaker, and a converted lampshade-turned-parabola. At a festival built on collective world-making, it's the object that makes the fiction feel real.

And yes, that is indeed a framed photo of the one and only Dolph Lundgren.
