Data Analytics for UX

Finding the stories inside the numbers — six findings on where and why users drop off, and what to do about it.

Role UX Researcher, Data Analyst
Tools Google Analytics 4, Hotjar, Lovable
Course Hyper Island UXUS8
Year 2026

Overview

This assessment applied a range of quantitative analytics methods to Gymgrossisten, one of the largest sports nutrition retailers in the Nordic market. The goal was to move beyond surface-level metrics and surface actionable UX insights from behavioural data — to understand not just what users were doing, but why, and what design decisions might be driving those behaviours.

Six friction points were identified across the site using bounce rate analysis, engagement time data, heatmaps, session recordings, and path exploration. Each finding pairs a data signal with an interpretation of the underlying behaviour and a recommendation for what to change.

Specific analytics figures are withheld under a confidentiality agreement with the client.

Finding 01 — Bounce rate data

You bounce pop-ups, I bounce... away from here

Object
/ (homepage)

Data
The homepage is the highest-traffic page on the site by a significant margin, yet shows a high bounce rate and very low average engagement time — a signal that users are arriving and leaving without acting.

Insight
First-time visitors are immediately presented with two simultaneous interruptions: a cookie consent banner and a membership popup, before they have seen any content or had any reason to engage. No buy-in equals no motivation to act.

Recommendations

  • Create a clearer visual hierarchy on the homepage using size, colour, and proportion — the page currently has no dominant entry point.
  • Reduce the cookie banner to a slim bottom bar rather than a centre-stage modal.
  • Delay the membership popup until after 30 seconds on site or first scroll.

Finding 02 — Engagement time data

20% (drop-) off

Object
/kosttillskott/proteinpulver

Data
The protein powder category is one of the highest-traffic pages on the site, yet shows a disproportionately high bounce rate and very low engagement time — unusually shallow behaviour for a high-intent product category.

Insight
The aggressive discount-first visual language — red "UPP TILL 20%" badge overlays and generic "Klicka här" CTAs — signals cheap and bargain-oriented rather than performance and quality. Users who identify as serious athletes or body-conscious consumers may disengage because the aesthetic doesn't match their self-image or aspirational identity.

Recommendation
Replace overlaid discount badge graphics with more integrated visual treatment, such as subtle percentage callouts within product cards. Replace "Klicka här" with specific CTAs like "Se proteinpulver" or "Utforska kollektionen".

Finding 03 — Heatmap

Ice ice baby

Object
Homepage heatmap

Data
The heatmap shows near-zero engagement below the Populära produkter section, despite significant page real estate devoted to brand partnerships, editorial content, and newsletter signup. The header area shows concentrated heat, but it is scattered — logo, search, and nav are all competing equally.

Insight
Users aren't scrolling far enough to reach the content investments below the fold. No single dominant entry point is drawing the eye, so users jump between competing elements rather than being guided through the page.

Recommendation
Restructure the page hierarchy. Move highest-converting elements — category navigation and top products — higher. Remove or collapse low-engagement sections. Increase the visual dominance of the search bar as a primary action.

Finding 04 — Path exploration

Where do I go? How do I get there?

Object
Path exploration of gymgrossisten.com

Data
A large volume of sessions start at the homepage, but only a small fraction reach checkout. /search is a top step-2 destination, meaning users are defaulting to search because the navigation isn't surfacing what they need.

Insight
Users are landing on the homepage without finding a clear route to purchase. The site doesn't guide them — it waits for them to figure it out themselves.

Recommendation
Add goal-oriented CTAs on the homepage — "Bygg muskler", "Gå ner i vikt", "Öka prestation" — that route users directly to relevant category pages and reduce the need to use search as a navigation fallback.

Finding 05 — Session recording

Wall of text

Object
/salte-elektrolyter-variety-28-pack/A19112-06.html, session recording

Data
The user is scrolling up and down several times across a product page presenting all product information as continuous unbroken text.

Insight
The scrolling behaviour suggests the user is trying to locate and cross-reference specific information — ingredients, dosage, usage instructions — that is buried within a dense block of copy. The cognitive load of holding information in memory while scanning forces repeated scrolling rather than straightforward reading.

Recommendation
Reduce cognitive overload, especially on mobile, by placing product information inside collapsible accordion sections — Produktbeskrivning, Näringsvärde & Ingredienser, Användning, Recensioner — so users can access what they need without losing their place.
