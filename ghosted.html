About

Intimacy and echo in virtual worlds

GHOSTED is an XR installation exploring intimacy and echo across parallel dimensions — physical space and cyberspace. It forms part of the larger project Metaphysical Archeology, which addresses the traces we leave behind as we move through digital worlds.

The installation exists in two forms simultaneously: a physical object in a forest glade, and a digital experience accessed through that object. A QR code is the threshold between them.

The Questions

What do we leave behind?

  • What traces do we leave behind when moving through the virtual world?
  • How real are the conversations and relations we initiate and experience online?
  • How do our words and actions online mirror our personas in the physical world?

The Physical Installation

A wall in the forest

A 3×2 metre plywood wall stands in a forest glade, detailed with laser-cut acrylic. Surrounding it: concrete gravestones. Visitors approach and find a QR code sign. They scan it with their phones — no app download required, compatible with iOS and Android — and the wall transforms.

Through the phone's camera, the wall reveals fragmented lost digital conversations. As viewers move through the space, different fragments appear. The AR layer maps onto the physical wall, so the experience is inseparable from standing in that specific place.

Technical Build

Built in Unity with AR Foundation

The AR experience was built in Unity using AR Foundation for cross-platform smartphone compatibility. AI image generation via Leonardo AI was used to create the visual fragments — ghostly conversation remnants that feel partially degraded, partially preserved.

The decision to require no app download was deliberate: friction is the enemy of encounter. If visitors have to install something, the forest moment is broken. The QR-to-browser pipeline keeps the threshold as thin as possible.

The forest is the frame. The wall is the archive. The phone is the key.

Status

Work in progress

GHOSTED is an ongoing work. The physical installation has been built and tested; the AR layer continues to develop. An online exposition version is also hosted at Research Catalogue for those who encounter the work outside the forest.
