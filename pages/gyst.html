GYST

How do you design an AI analytics tool that makes a million-dollar content creator feel smart, not stupid?

Role UX/UI Design, User Flow, Persona Development
Tools Figma, ProtoPie, Photoshop
Year 2026

Overview

Redesigning onboarding for the overwhelmed digital creative

GYST is an AI-powered revenue intelligence tool for elite content creators — a conversational CFO that translates complex business data from multiple platforms into plain language insights. The redesign challenge focused on first-time user onboarding: welcoming someone brilliant at building but deeply intimidated by data.

This case study documents how accessibility, narrative structure, and emotional trust can reshape a product's first impression — and what I learned about cognitive ease along the way.

The Problem

Built for analysts, not creators

Top 5% content creators manage revenue across eight or more platforms simultaneously. Yet existing analytics tools are static, text-heavy, and cognitively demanding — built for data analysts, not people who built a business through instinct, creativity, and hustle.

Three critical friction points emerged:

  • Cognitive overload at first contact — too much information before users understand the value
  • No clear value moment — setup requires effort before anything useful appears
  • Platform fragmentation — connecting data sources feels burdensome and technical

User Persona

Meet Jess

Jess is a US-based creator-operator running a multi-platform media and wellness business generating between $10M and $500M annually with a small, high-leverage team. She makes decisions about pricing, sponsorships, and strategy — often without complete data. She's not analytically trained. She's dyslexic. And she built something remarkable while quietly feeling uncertain about her own business.

"i built a million-dollar business but feel stupid looking at a spreadsheet."

Jess became the design compass. Every decision was filtered through one question: does this make Jess feel capable, or behind?

Design Solution

Three-stage onboarding: earn trust before asking for effort

The redesign restructured onboarding around progressive disclosure — showing value before requesting configuration.

  • Stage 1 — Soft setup: Connect platforms, get data flowing. Immediate value before any customisation.
  • Stage 2 — Questionnaire: Five focused, skippable questions that educate both Jess and GYST simultaneously.
  • Stage 3 — Deep setup: A meaningful overview, now informed by real data from her actual business.

Process

From friction map to functioning prototype

The process began with an AS-IS user journey map — identifying every moment where Jess would encounter confusion, doubt, or friction. From there, a TO-BE storyboard was developed, tracing her improved path from discovery to her first genuine insight. This narrative became the project's foundational spine.

The storyboard was then translated into a functional user flow in Figma: decision points, skip logic, platform connection states, and error handling. In parallel, a handoff-ready design system was developed — variables, tokens, semantic colour naming, light and dark variants.

Copy was treated as a design material. GYST's core promise — "speaks human, not spreadsheet" — was carried through every line: buttons, error states, empty states, onboarding prompts. "Connect your platforms" instead of "Integrate data sources." "What matters most to you?" instead of "Select primary KPIs."

Deliverables

What was built

  • Mid-fidelity Figma mockup of the full onboarding flow
  • Handoff-ready design system with variables, tokens, and semantic colour naming
  • Interactive ProtoPie prototype demonstrating the complete interface
  • User journey maps (AS-IS and TO-BE)
  • Persona documentation

Outcome

Making the complex feel human

Presented in February 2026, the redesign demonstrated how emotional trust and cognitive ease can fundamentally reframe a product's first impression. By treating onboarding as a conversation rather than a configuration task, the friction between Jess and her own data dissolves.

"I actually understand my business now."
