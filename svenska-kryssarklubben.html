UX Research & Experimentation — 2026

Svenska Kryssarklubben

How do you redesign a 100-year-old sailing club's digital experience for people who have never set foot on a boat?

Role User research, User interviews, Synthesis, Prototype testing
Tools Figma, Miro, Lovable
Context Hyper Island UX Experimentation Course
Year 2026

Overview

Making sailing feel like it's for someone like you

Svenska Kryssarklubben is one of the world's largest sailing organizations. Founded in 1923, it has tens of thousands of members — almost none of them under 30. The website was built in 2013 and shows it. As part of a UX Experimentation course at Hyper Island, our team of five investigated why younger audiences weren't converting and tested whether a redesigned digital experience could change that.

My role was generalist across the project — research design, interviews, synthesis, and usability testing.

The Problem

Built for boat owners, not the boat-curious

The existing sxk.se assumes you already own a boat. The navigation, the copy, the imagery, the membership benefits — all of it speaks to someone already inside the world. For a first-time visitor with no sailing background, the site answers one question immediately: this isn't for you.

Three friction points emerged:

  • Visual language that signals exclusivity before a single word is read
  • No social proof that people like you exist in the club
  • A try-before-you-join path so buried it effectively doesn't exist

Research

Listening before designing

We conducted semi-structured interviews using Mom Test principles — asking about past behavior rather than hypothetical preferences. Participants navigated the existing site while thinking out loud. We also built an expert review identifying eight pain points through the lens of a single persona: the Curious Non-Sailor, aged 20–30, no boat, no background, genuinely interested.

"I can't afford a boat. And I don't have the knowledge."
"It feels very much like something for upper middle class in Sweden."
"We believe the sea should be open to everyone" — read aloud directly against imagery that said otherwise.

Synthesis

From observations to assumptions

We mapped 16 assumptions by evidence level and effort to test, surfacing four that would most affect design direction. From these we built a persona — Filippa, 24, sustainability consultant, mobile-first, drawn to the lifestyle but stopped at the door. Her question: is this for someone like me?

The site had no answer.

Design & Testing

Does the fix actually work?

The team built a high-fidelity prototype with a visible beginner entry path, refreshed imagery, and cleaner navigation. We ran a second round of usability tests against it.

What worked: the visual refresh landed immediately — 4 of 5 participants read the redesign as more energetic and accessible. The entry path was discoverable without prompting for 3 of 5 participants.

What didn't: path labels confused everyone who used them. Secondary pages reintroduced the commitment asks the homepage had successfully deferred. The chatbot was missed or actively avoided by all five participants.

The hypothesis was inconclusive — partly supported, but all participants were aged 33–41. The 20–30 persona has not yet been tested.

Recommendations

Three things SXK should do next

Extend the tonal direction — it's working, apply it everywhere. Rename the entry paths — discoverable isn't enough if the labels don't land. And close the representation gap — a site that says "open to everyone" while showing a narrow demographic doesn't say open to everyone. It says the opposite.

One bigger picture note: younger audiences increasingly discover organizations through AI answers and short-form social, not search. A better website is step one. Showing up before anyone visits the site is step two.

Outcome

The most useful thing an experiment can give you is a better question

Testing is not a one-time event. Our two rounds of research told different stories that only made sense together. The most honest output wasn't a confirmed hypothesis — it was knowing exactly what to test next.

What happens when we run this with someone who's actually 24?
